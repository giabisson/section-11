# Cycling coaching update plan

Prepared 22 October 2026; status updated 23 October 2026 for branch `codex/cycling-coaching-literature`.

The highest-value rework is to make training decisions depend on the athlete's goals, feasible training, completed work and response, with a durable record of what changed and why. Preserve the reusable workout library, deterministic calculations, selective data retrieval and existing calendar-write protections. More LLM-generated workouts would not by itself improve coaching.

This document records the coaching implementation and its evidence limits. The seven steps are implemented and deployed for the primary account; the later priority sections preserve the original rationale and acceptance criteria. The evidence base is the [17 paper takeaways](literature/cycling-training/TAKEAWAYS.md), including their access and methodological limits. The recommendations apply across time budgets and experience levels; the athlete's current availability should determine the schedule.

**Design requirement:** overnight HRV may be intermittent by choice while RHR is available more often. Sleep comfort takes priority over collecting wearable data. Current self-reported state is useful coaching evidence, not a second-class substitute that must wait for an HRV baseline. Do not require nightly wear, manufacture normal missing values or let favorable feedback erase actual warning signals. Use existing training context and ask only unresolved decision-relevant questions.

## Evidence rules for the update

**Evidence-supported practice:** structure training beyond isolated sessions; include recovery and specificity; observe completed work, performance and athlete feedback; consider multiple intensity distributions; individualize event preparation. The strength and limits of each claim remain those in the linked takeaways.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** the proposed planning schema, review cadence, question selection, conservative progression and rules for rearranging sessions. These are design choices to evaluate, not algorithms directly validated by the papers.

**Unsupported claims to remove or narrow:** a universal 80/20 requirement, a universally superior periodization pattern, a scientifically validated composite readiness index, universal safe load bands, or a performance guarantee from a studied workout. The curated literature does not validate those claims. Other existing thresholds need an evidence audit rather than an assumption that every unreviewed rule is false.

## Priority 1 Make readiness and health decisions explicit

**Initial finding and current status.** The review found that wellness array position could stand in for today's date, baseline depth was opaque, and gaps could be counted as persistent physiological changes. Step 2 corrects those inputs and exposes dated provenance/coverage. Active P2 still counts overlapping HRV, RHR and RI observations separately; the audit-only grouped candidate records disagreements for later review. Step 1 qualified the physiological `go`, and Step 2 carries the health clarification flag alongside it; the external coach must still apply current feedback and the health rules. These changes do not validate the legacy RI or its thresholds. See [dated observation implementation](examples/sync.py) and the completed-step notes above.

**Evidence.** [Manresa-Rocamora](literature/cycling-training/papers/34639599/takeaways.md) supports methodological consistency and contextual interpretation, without establishing universal cutoffs or clear performance superiority. [Saw](literature/cycling-training/papers/26423706/takeaways.md) supports retaining subjective observations individually. [Meeusen](literature/cycling-training/papers/23247672/takeaways.md) does not support diagnosis from one marker. [Alfonso](literature/cycling-training/papers/41028151/takeaways.md) is promising but does not validate the repository's composite. The [2019 Javaloyes record](literature/cycling-training/papers/29809080/takeaways.md) remains abstract-only; the [2020 experiment](literature/cycling-training/papers/31490431/takeaways.md) is a distinct study with no established between-group performance advantage.

**Changes.**

1. Add observation dates, baseline sample counts/coverage, metric identity, measurement source and method-change flags where available. Unknown provenance stays unknown. Define the reference window explicitly and test sparse dates: array position alone must not establish consecutive-day persistence. Use a baseline that is suitable for its stated purpose; no baseline duration in this reading set is universally optimal.
2. Separate data quality from the physiological recommendation. Introduce an explicit insufficient-context status or a required follow-up field in a versioned decision contract. Missing HRV alone should not prevent training when a known plan, adequate training history and current feedback support a decision; it must not count as evidence of good recovery.
3. Review correlated signal counting. Retain RI as a labeled heuristic/legacy field while comparing a candidate policy that groups related physiological observations rather than treating them as independent votes. Do not describe a new formula as validated simply because it is deterministic.
4. Produce a final coaching decision that combines the physiological output, existing `health_context`, current athlete feedback and the proposed session. Record unresolved questions and the basis for proceeding, modifying or skipping. Preserve the existing rule that health clarification cannot relax a Skip, and that a marker alone is neither recovery confirmation nor an automatic Skip.
5. Use a short, consistently defined check-in for decision-relevant fatigue, soreness, stress, sleep/recovery and symptoms. Keep timestamps, scales and individual answers. Do not infer current Feel from a completed activity's Feel, or treat a bespoke questionnaire as validated.

**Files.** Start with `_calculate_derived_metrics`, `_compute_readiness_decision` and `_build_health_context` in `examples/sync.py`; align the readiness, progression and health sections in `SECTION_11.md`, both `PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS_*.md`, `SKILL.md`, JSON examples and pre-workout reporting.

**Acceptance.** Cases include all optional wearable fields missing; one HRV observation; stale wellness; nonconsecutive dates; RMSSD/SDNN or device changes; reassuring physiology with current symptoms; a recent marker with unknown recovery; and partial marker coverage. No case may turn absence of data into physiological reassurance. Run the candidate policy in comparison mode before replacing existing decisions; retain existing safeguards during migration unless a separately reviewed change justifies alteration.

## Priority 2 Replace universal planning rules with athlete-specific policy

**Initial finding and current status.** The initial review found universal volume, intensity-ratio and session-count requirements, age/RI-selected block sketches and long-ride gates that could override the athlete's actual goal and capacity. Step 3 replaces these with the athlete-specific policy in [Section 11 B](SECTION_11.md#11-b-ai-training-plan-protocol), contextual [progression evidence](SECTION_11.md#progression-evidence) and optional [library block sketches](examples/workout-library/WORKOUT_REFERENCE.md#4-block-periodisation-sketches). The policy and examples define coaching intent. The managed plan path now enforces schema, prescription and session/day/week capacity constraints; the typed coaching bridge supplies current safety/source context. These guards do not establish truthful athlete inputs or optimal training decisions, and standalone push calls do not enforce the whole plan policy.

**Evidence.** [Galán-Rioja](literature/cycling-training/papers/36640771/takeaways.md) finds no consistently superior cycling periodization model. [Rosenblat](literature/cycling-training/papers/39888556/takeaways.md) finds no clear overall polarized-versus-pyramidal winner; a session-goal split is not equivalent to recorded minutes. [Neal](literature/cycling-training/papers/23264537/takeaways.md) supports purposeful training comparisons, with unequal load and unresolved erratum limits; it cannot establish a universal ratio.

**Changes.** Replace universal defaults with an explicit policy selected from goals, recent completed training, experience, current capacity, stable constraints and dated availability. Derive a proposed weekly budget from those inputs; do not fill missing history with 15 hours. Make build/recovery patterns and the number of demanding sessions adjustable planning choices. Retain useful conservative progression, but label exact percentage ramps and RI/load bands as heuristics unless directly substantiated.

Name the zone system, intensity basis, aggregation method and period in every distribution summary. The existing seven-to-three-zone conversion assumes boundaries ([sync.py:10948](examples/sync.py#L10948)); expose that assumption rather than claiming individually measured LT1/LT2. Preserve existing `zone_basis`, including `mixed`, and avoid treating mixed power/HR aggregates as an exact physiological prescription. Distinguish seven-zone Z3 from three-zone Z3. Replace blanket weekly “grey zone <5%” and “Z4+ drives adaptation” wording in [WEEKLY_REPORT_TEMPLATE.md:127](examples/reports/WEEKLY_REPORT_TEMPLATE.md#L127) with interpretation relative to the chosen objective.

Create a compact claim/rule register recording source, population/outcome, evidence versus coaching judgment, limitations and policy version. Narrow the blanket validation language in [SECTION_11.md:761](SECTION_11.md#L761). Reframe the optional fasted-Z2 progression ([1708](SECTION_11.md#L1708)): favorable RI/HRV is not sufficient justification, and enhanced metabolic flexibility should not imply established performance benefit. [Gejl and Nybo](literature/cycling-training/papers/34001184/takeaways.md) do not establish a general performance advantage. Exact fueling, heat and altitude prescriptions require their own evidence review.

**Acceptance.** Use contrasting synthetic athletes with limited, moderate and high availability, different experience and goals, and no wearable requirement. The same objective may yield different schedules. Sensible moderate-intensity work must not fail solely because it is not polarized. Missing two-hour rides must not block unrelated progression. Calendar season or age alone must not determine the block pattern.

## Priority 3 Store a coherent plan and its revisions

**Initial finding and current status.** The [plan header:3428](SECTION_11.md#L3428) already contains phase, load and session selections, but does not establish a persistent longitudinal planning system. Current calendar entries and saved-workout inventory cannot reliably answer what an earlier plan prescribed after edits. An inferred phase describes training patterns; it does not establish the athlete's intended progression or event strategy.

**Evidence.** [Galán-Rioja](literature/cycling-training/papers/36640771/takeaways.md) supports evaluating coherent training structure without promising one superior model. The following storage design is engineering and coaching judgment.

**Implemented in Step 5.** The [versioned plan contract and CLI](examples/agentic/COACHING_PLANS.md) store private goals, block intent, dated availability, immutable prescriptions, revisions and publication history. The canonical `coaching_plan.sqlite3` transaction journal replaces the initially proposed independently written `coaching_plan.json`/`coaching_decisions.jsonl` pair: proposal/snapshot commits and pre-send operation records must remain atomic after a crash. JSON remains the interchange/read format. Personal records live in an explicitly configured private directory outside the source/update checkout. The primary deployment now configures that directory and its history reader explicitly; it has not created a real athlete plan.

| Level | Required planning information |
|---|---|
| Goal or event horizon | Goal, priority, date where applicable, demands, success measure and known constraints; general fitness is a valid goal |
| Training block | Objective, starting evidence, planned progression, recovery opportunities, review date and conditions for changing course |
| Week | Dated availability, intended sessions, time budget, chosen intensity approach and explanation of compromises |
| Session | Stable prescription identity, template and version, exact adapted steps, intensity reference, expected duration, purpose and permitted alternatives |
| Revision | Previous version, reason, supporting observations, affected future sessions, publication state and external calendar identity where available |

Preserve the dossier's stable-context boundary ([DOSSIER_TEMPLATE.md:248](DOSSIER_TEMPLATE.md#L248)); temporary availability and current wellness belong in dated planning inputs. Store planned intent separately from detected phase. A contradiction should prompt review, not silently rewrite the goal. Keep long-range plans flexible and progressively specify near-term sessions.

Maintain immutable prescription snapshots, including resolved targets and their dated reference values, before calendar publication. Track proposed, confirmed, published and uncertain outcomes separately. Keep the existing preview/confirmation flow and unique external IDs; reconcile an uncertain write before retrying. Do not infer completion from a successful publication.

**Acceptance.** A fresh agent session can recover the current plan, its rationale and the prescription applicable to an old completed session. Changing a saved workout or current FTP cannot retroactively alter that snapshot. A changed race date or weekly availability creates an attributable revision. Plan validation runs before the existing calendar preview; validation is distinct from authorization to publish.

## Priority 4 Connect completion and feedback to future prescriptions

**Finding and consequence.** Existing [feedback loops:3271](SECTION_11.md#L3271) and [weekly compliance reporting:125](examples/reports/WEEKLY_REPORT_TEMPLATE.md#L125) express the right intent. Their value depends on carrying the paired prescription and resulting adjustment into the next planning decision, rather than producing another metrics report.

**Evidence.** [Saw](literature/cycling-training/papers/26423706/takeaways.md), [Meeusen](literature/cycling-training/papers/23247672/takeaways.md) and [Manresa-Rocamora](literature/cycling-training/papers/34639599/takeaways.md) support monitoring the athlete's response with limits on automated interpretation. The precise adjustment process below is proposed coaching judgment.

**Implemented in Step 6 and primary runtime integration.** The shared review store, typed coaching tool and scheduled supervisor implement the following contract; synthetic/native SDK checks verify persistence and recovery, while the first real managed review awaits onboarding. Match completed activities to prescriptions using authoritative links where available; preserve ambiguous matches as unresolved. Retain the existing formal mapping gate for compliance and the separation between adherence, goal attainment and response cost. Ask why a session changed when that answer affects the next recommendation: time, illness, fatigue, equipment and preference require different responses.

At each relevant review, choose explicitly among maintaining, progressing, reducing, substituting, rescheduling or requesting context. Record the rationale and the next observation that would support another change. A missed session creates no automatic debt, overload catch-up or FTP reduction. Repeated inability to tolerate a dose triggers review of fitness estimates, recovery, placement and fueling before escalation. Replace diagnostic “overreach” language based solely on an RPE change ([SECTION_11.md:1724](SECTION_11.md#L1724)) with a contextual fatigue response.

Weekly reviews should state what was intended, what happened, the athlete's response and the resulting future changes. Block reviews should evaluate the objective's relevant performance and sustainability, not CTL/VO2 alone. Add revision IDs and unresolved questions to existing report templates. Scheduled reviews remain opt-in in the external runtime; persist the last reviewed data/plan version so the same evidence does not repeatedly trigger changes or messages.

**Acceptance.** Replay a multiweek scenario containing a successful block, unusually difficult intervals, a missed ride due to work, reduced availability, symptoms and an event-date change. Future sessions must reflect those causes, retain a coherent objective and expose uncertainty. Re-running the same review must not create duplicate revisions or calendar writes. Validate both athlete-requested and configured scheduled review paths.

## Priority 5 Validate exact workout dose and fitness provenance

**Initial finding and current status.** The initial push path validated basic fields and native syntax without checking complete dose, template constraints or the intensity denominator. Step 4 adds the [shared structured compiler](examples/agentic/workout_validation.py) and [checked catalog](examples/workout-library/workout_constraints.json), used before preview and confirmed creation. It covers all 26 library templates with explicit scope limits; unknown formats fail structured checking and native inputs remain labeled `not_checked`. Athlete/health suitability, exact technique, supplied-source truth and whole-plan feasibility remain outside those numeric guards. See the completed-step notes and [validation contract](examples/agentic/WORKOUT_VALIDATION.md).

**Evidence.** [Seiler](literature/cycling-training/papers/21812820/takeaways.md), [Rønnestad](literature/cycling-training/papers/24382021/takeaways.md) and [Little](literature/cycling-training/papers/20100740/takeaways.md) show why work, rest, effort and reference power matter. Their protocols are options, not a requirement to reproduce them for every athlete. [Wong](literature/cycling-training/papers/36803419/takeaways.md) cautions against equating one FTP estimate with a measured physiological boundary.

**Changes.** Represent selected workouts as structured steps before rendering Intervals.icu descriptions. Calculate full duration, including warm-up, cool-down, within-set and between-set recoveries, with explicit handling of whether the final recovery exists. Check time windows and permitted template adaptations deterministically. Keep estimated load labeled as an estimate; do not invent precise TSS from a session label.

Attach source/protocol references and deviations to templates. Preserve unresolved source discrepancies rather than claiming exact reproduction; the Rønnestad paper's recovery descriptions conflict. Keep FTP, ramp peak power, critical power and measured thresholds distinct. Extend the existing [FTP timeline:14948](examples/sync.py#L14948) and [testing protocol:2661](SECTION_11.md#L2661) with method, date, equipment and uncertainty where obtainable. Unknown test method remains unknown. Reconcile stale or contradictory estimates with repeatable performance and effort; do not substitute a universal new FTP multiplier or trigger maximal testing when health makes it inappropriate.

**Acceptance.** A selected short-interval variant's total duration includes every actual recovery and preparation step. An over-budget session is shortened within permitted variants or replaced with an explained alternative. A ramp-peak percentage cannot silently become the same FTP percentage. Tests cover relative and absolute saved targets, stale fitness estimates, unknown methods and incomplete workout structures. Keep template reuse; new formats require a reviewed library addition rather than unconstrained LLM invention.

## Event-specific improvements (implemented and deployed for primary; real-use validation pending)

**Event preparation and tapering.** The original protocol used fixed taper/CTL budgets and inferred event length from priority. Step 7 replaces those defaults with `event_specific_v1`, [explicit plan context](examples/agentic/EVENT_PREPARATION.md), supplied completed-training evidence, actual duration/demands, rationale and arithmetic against full selected prescriptions. Missing duration remains unknown. [Wang](literature/cycling-training/papers/37163550/takeaways.md) supports useful tapering without a universal subgroup optimum. Tests cover short/long priority events, missing duration, low reference volume and mismatched taper dose. Active readiness/health still applies.

**Durability.** Optional paired power-window context now preserves prior duration, intensity, mechanical work, pacing, fueling, environment and conditions. Unknown or incompatible context yields unavailable comparison; mechanical kJ cannot become dietary kcal. Existing decoupling gates remain separate. [Sánchez-Jiménez](literature/cycling-training/papers/40613880/takeaways.md) supplies acute context, not a universal fatigue-work threshold or proven long-term training formula. No exhaustive testing or long ride is required for unrelated progression.

**Strength.** Optional context records goal, experience, equipment, technique, soreness, addition versus replacement and recovery. Timed strength counts with other sports in availability; low-cadence cycling is distinct from heavy resistance. [Llanos-Lagos](literature/cycling-training/papers/40632222/takeaways.md) retains low certainty and no guaranteed gain or universal routine. Tests cover replacement, over-budget additions and unknown preparation. Exercise technique/suitability and live runtime remain outside those arithmetic checks.
