# Coaching evidence and policy register

This register supports `athlete_specific_v1` in Section 11 B. It records what the reviewed literature can support and which application rules remain coaching judgment. Read the linked takeaways when a claim needs explanation; routine coaching does not require loading every paper. The collection is curated, not a complete systematic review of all coaching topics.

| Topic | Evidence and limits | Application rule and status |
|---|---|---|
| Planning over weeks | [Galán-Rioja](literature/cycling-training/papers/36640771/takeaways.md): small, heterogeneous cycling studies; no consistently superior periodization model and little seasonal comparative evidence | Give blocks an objective, progression/recovery opportunities and review points. The schema, review cadence and any 2:1/3:1/4:1 choice are coaching judgment, not proven optimal patterns. |
| Intensity distribution | [Rosenblat](literature/cycling-training/papers/39888556/takeaways.md): no clear overall polarized-versus-pyramidal advantage; session intent and recorded time can differ. [Neal](literature/cycling-training/papers/23264537/takeaways.md): informative small comparison with unequal volume/load and unresolved erratum | Choose distribution by objective, training history, time and response. Universal 80/20 validation, blanket Z3 limits and mandatory hard-session counts are not justified. Name the zone model, basis and window. |
| Training volume and progression | [Galán-Rioja](literature/cycling-training/papers/36640771/takeaways.md), [Meeusen](literature/cycling-training/papers/23247672/takeaways.md): structure and individualized recovery matter; these sources do not establish a universally safe weekly ramp | Use completed training as a reference and availability as a ceiling. No default hours or compulsory increase. Exact increments, spacing and review bands are explainable coaching heuristics. |
| Workout dose | [Seiler](literature/cycling-training/papers/21812820/takeaways.md), [Rønnestad](literature/cycling-training/papers/24382021/takeaways.md), [Little](literature/cycling-training/papers/20100740/takeaways.md): particular interval programs can improve selected outcomes; limited samples/durations and important protocol differences | Keep reusable templates and individual variants. Neither a studied workout nor a short-session option proves a whole plan, universal superiority or replacement of all event-specific volume. The [checked compiler](examples/agentic/WORKOUT_VALIDATION.md) verifies documented numeric constraints for all 26 templates, not scientific efficacy or exact reproduction of every paper. |
| Persistent planning | [Galán-Rioja](literature/cycling-training/papers/36640771/takeaways.md): evaluate training structure without claiming one superior periodization model | The [private plan store](examples/agentic/COACHING_PLANS.md) persists intent, supplied availability, immutable prescriptions and explained revisions. Its schema, transaction journal and capacity checks are engineering choices; passing tests establishes those behaviors, not effective adaptation or training outcomes. Publication is distinct from completion. |
| Athlete feedback and wearables | [Saw](literature/cycling-training/papers/26423706/takeaways.md): subjective measures can be responsive, without validating every bespoke questionnaire. [Manresa-Rocamora](literature/cycling-training/papers/34639599/takeaways.md): heterogeneous HRV methods, no robust performance superiority | Keep feedback meaningful and wearables optional. Dates, coverage and unknown measurement provenance matter. RI formula/cutoffs, signal-count groups and Feel thresholds are application heuristics, not validated clinical boundaries. Grouped counts remain audit-only. |
| Recovery and health | [Meeusen](literature/cycling-training/papers/23247672/takeaways.md): no single biomarker diagnoses overtraining; persistent problems need context and possibly clinical assessment | Preserve active readiness/health protections while reviewing their empirical support. CTL/TSB, ACWR, monotony and a favorable self-report are not injury probabilities or medical clearance. This collection is not current condition-specific return guidance. |
| Nutrition progression | [Gejl and Nybo](literature/cycling-training/papers/34001184/takeaways.md): short carbohydrate-restriction studies did not establish an overall performance advantage | Do not make fasted riding a default performance step or authorize it from RI/HRV alone. Exact fueling doses and energy-availability guidance need separate evidence. |
| Event demands and additional training | [Wang](literature/cycling-training/papers/37163550/takeaways.md), [Sánchez-Jiménez](literature/cycling-training/papers/40613880/takeaways.md), [Llanos-Lagos](literature/cycling-training/papers/40632222/takeaways.md): taper benefit with uncertain optimal settings, acute durability context, and low-certainty strength benefits | Individualize tapering, durability assessment and strength within time/recovery budgets. Optional [event/durability/strength context](examples/agentic/EVENT_PREPARATION.md) now records these choices in the same plan/capacity contract. Descriptive context and arithmetic do not establish an optimal dose or guarantee performance. |

The literature does not validate the application as a whole. Failure to establish a difference is not proof of equivalence. Reviews and their included experiments are not independent replications. Keep each takeaway's population, outcome, access and methodological limits with the claim.

Legacy protocol tables contain additional references and numerical rules not fully assessed in this collection. Do not call them scientifically validated because an author's name is attached. Preserve existing protective decisions during review, identify the applicable policy/version and distinguish descriptive load bands from safety claims. Method/device-change detection, clinical return rules and detailed nutrition/environmental prescriptions need their own evidence before stronger automation claims.

The legacy ACWR string `optimal` is a compatibility label for a reporting band, not an empirically established optimum or permission to train. This update does not change the active readiness ladder or thresholds.
