## Why it belongs

An influential example of adaptations from brief cycling sessions, useful when time is constrained but easily overgeneralized.

## Findings — full HTML text, no original PDF

Seven young men completed six sessions over two weeks in an uncontrolled pre/post study. Training progressed from eight to ten to twelve 60-second efforts across pairs of sessions, with 75-second low-power recoveries. Effort power corresponded to peak power from a ramp oxygen-uptake test. Sessions took about 20–29 minutes including the specified warm-up and recoveries. Performance and several skeletal-muscle mitochondrial markers improved. Familiarization trials were stable before training.

## Interpretation and limits

This was not a randomized comparison with an endurance-training group, and it was not a long-term study of trained competitive cyclists. Molecular adaptation does not demonstrate an optimal season plan, nor that short intervals reproduce every adaptation needed for a long event. Familiarization reduces one concern but does not substitute for a control group.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** short sessions can be productive, and work/recovery structure and progression matter. This is an option within a plan, not a reason to make every session intense.

**Critical intensity distinction:** approximately 100% of ramp-test peak power is not 100% FTP. Directly substituting those denominators changes the exercise substantially.
