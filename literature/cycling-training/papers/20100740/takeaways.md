# Coach takeaways: Little et al. (2010): low-volume high-intensity cycling

**Paper:** A practical model of low-volume high-intensity interval training induces mitochondrial biogenesis in human skeletal muscle: potential mechanisms.

[Study / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2009.181743) · [Detailed assessment](notes.md) · [All paper takeaways](../../TAKEAWAYS.md)

**Evidence basis:** Full HTML/XML text reviewed; original PDF unavailable. Prepared 22 October 2026.

## Main coaching lesson

**Short sessions can be productive when they provide a purposeful training stimulus.**

## What the paper adds

Seven young men completed six sessions over two weeks in an uncontrolled pre/post study. Sessions progressed from eight to twelve 60-second efforts, with 75-second recoveries, taking about 20–29 minutes. Power corresponded to the peak reached in a ramp oxygen-uptake test. Performance and several muscle mitochondrial markers improved; familiarization trials were stable before training.

## Coaching application

These are practical coaching interpretations, not a directly tested decision algorithm.

- Keep shorter session options available when time, adherence or circumstances make a longer session impractical.
- Preserve the session's intended stimulus, suitable preparation and recoveries when adapting its duration.
- Evaluate progress with riding performance and tolerability rather than inferring success from a workout's molecular rationale.

## Limits on the takeaway

The study was short, small and lacked a randomized endurance-training comparator. It does not demonstrate that this approach replaces long-ride preparation, remains optimal over months, or suits every athlete. Molecular adaptation is not the same endpoint as event performance. Approximately 100% of ramp-test peak power is not 100% FTP; substituting the denominator changes the workout substantially.

## Implication for a coaching application

Keep intensity definitions and the reason for each session explicit. Offer shorter alternatives within a coherent plan while preserving uncertainty about which adaptations transfer to the athlete's goal. These are proposed requirements, not claims about implemented repository behavior.
