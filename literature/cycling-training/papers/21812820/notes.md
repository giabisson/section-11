## Why it belongs

Shows how interval intensity and accumulated work interact, rather than treating the hardest effort as automatically the best dose.

## Findings — full paper, manually supplied

Thirty-seven recreational cyclists were recruited; 35 were analyzed (29 men, 6 women), with nine per interval group. Seven weeks of twice-weekly 4×4, 4×8, or 4×16-minute sessions were compared with lower-intensity-only training. All participants had prior interval experience. The 4×8 group achieved the greatest combined improvement across the reported aerobic outcomes. The often-quoted 11.4% is an average across three outcomes, not a guaranteed gain in one performance measure.

The verified prescription was maximal sustainable effort across the session: 4×8 and 4×4 used **two-minute recoveries**, while 4×16 used three minutes. Sessions began with a 15-minute warm-up. Additional endurance sessions were guided below the first ventilatory threshold. Average heart-rate responses describe achieved intensity, not a fixed percentage-of-FTP target.

## Interpretation and limits

Groups were small; the 4×8 group happened to contain only men. Allocation included swaps for scheduling, the lower-intensity control was unsupervised, training volume increased in the control/4×16 groups, and residual post-intervention fatigue may have differed. The performance test was time to exhaustion at a fixed pre-training workload, not a race time trial. Anecdotal difficulty tolerating two hard sessions is not a validated population-wide safety limit.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** use 4×8 as one option when suitable, progress tolerable work, and assess recovery. Do not prescribe it universally or assume its hard-session frequency suits everyone.
