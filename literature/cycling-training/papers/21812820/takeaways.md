# Coach takeaways: Seiler et al. (2013; online 2011): interval intensity and duration

**Paper:** Adaptations to aerobic interval training: interactive effects of exercise intensity and total work duration.

[Study / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2011.01351.x) · [Detailed assessment](notes.md) · [All paper takeaways](../../TAKEAWAYS.md)

**Evidence basis:** Full paper assessed; selected original-PDF figures, tables or equations checked. See the detailed assessment for scope and unresolved issues. Prepared 22 October 2026.

## Main coaching lesson

**Balance work duration and intensity so the athlete can accumulate useful, repeatable interval work.**

## What the paper adds

Thirty-five recreationally trained cyclists were analyzed after seven weeks of lower-intensity-only training or twice-weekly 4×4, 4×8 or 4×16-minute intervals plus easy training. The 4×8 group achieved the greatest combined improvement across the reported aerobic outcomes. Intervals were prescribed at maximal sustainable session effort: 4×8 and 4×4 used two-minute recoveries; 4×16 used three minutes. The reported heart rates describe achieved responses.

## Coaching application

These are practical coaching interpretations, not a directly tested decision algorithm.

- Offer 4×8 as one possible session for a suitably prepared cyclist, with its purpose, recovery and intensity basis stated.
- Consider work completed, power consistency, perceived effort and subsequent recovery when choosing or progressing a session.
- When an athlete struggles, reconsider the dose and placement of the session before assuming that more intensity will produce a better result.

## Limits on the takeaway

The groups were small and the 4×8 group contained only men. The result does not establish a universal best workout or prove that everyone should perform two hard sessions weekly. The often-cited 11.4% improvement averages three outcomes; it is not a guaranteed performance gain. The integrated performance test was time to exhaustion, and residual fatigue may have influenced post-testing. An achieved heart rate is not a fixed percentage-of-FTP prescription.

## Implication for a coaching application

Represent repetitions, work duration, recoveries, intensity denominator and session effort separately. Progression should respond to completed training and recovery; a protocol match alone does not validate the surrounding plan. These are proposed requirements, not claims about implemented repository behavior.
