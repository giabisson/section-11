# Performance effects of periodized carbohydrate restriction in endurance trained athletes - a systematic review and meta-analysis

[Practical coach takeaways](takeaways.md)

Gejl KD, Nybo L. Performance effects of periodized carbohydrate restriction in endurance trained athletes - a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2021;18(1):37. doi: 10.1186/s12970-021-00435-3

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34001184/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-021-00435-3) · [Metadata](metadata.json) · [Abstract](abstract.txt)

**Evidence basis:** full paper pdf. Reviewed 22 October 2026.

## Why it belongs

Tests the performance claim behind deliberate low-carbohydrate training strategies, rather than assuming a molecular signal guarantees better riding.

## Findings — full paper

Nine studies of trained endurance athletes examined 1–4 weeks of periodized carbohydrate restriction, including fasted, sleep-low, and twice-daily approaches. The pooled performance effect did not establish superiority over higher-carbohydrate controls: SMD 0.17, 95% CI −0.15 to 0.49, p = .29. Two studies reported favorable within-group findings without reporting group-by-time interactions.

## Interpretation and limits

Small, short studies, mostly male participants, different strategies, and a fixed-effect synthesis limit certainty. A confidence interval spanning zero is not proof of no possible benefit. Conversely, mechanistic adaptations are not evidence that the athlete will perform better. The authors' discussion of possible benefits in lower-volume training is not a demonstrated subgroup effect.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** avoid defaulting to fasted or carbohydrate-restricted sessions as a requirement for improvement. Consider whether food availability supports session quality, recovery, and the athlete's broader health and preferences.

**Not justified:** universal train-low recommendations, promises of superior endurance from fasting, or exact grams-per-hour fueling rules derived from this review. A dedicated nutrition guideline is needed for those doses.
