# Coach takeaways: Gejl and Nybo (2021): periodized carbohydrate restriction

**Paper:** Performance effects of periodized carbohydrate restriction in endurance trained athletes - a systematic review and meta-analysis.

[Study / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-021-00435-3) · [Detailed assessment](notes.md) · [All paper takeaways](../../TAKEAWAYS.md)

**Evidence basis:** Full paper assessed; selected original-PDF figures, tables or equations checked. See the detailed assessment for scope and unresolved issues. Prepared 22 October 2026.

## Main coaching lesson

**Make carbohydrate restriction a deliberate, evidence-limited choice rather than a routine performance requirement.**

## What the paper adds

Nine short studies in trained endurance athletes compared periodized carbohydrate restriction with higher-carbohydrate approaches. Strategies included fasted, sleep-low and twice-daily training. The pooled performance effect did not establish superiority: standardized effect 0.17, 95% confidence interval −0.15 to 0.49. Mechanistic adaptations did not establish a reliable performance advantage.

## Coaching application

These are practical coaching interpretations, not a directly tested decision algorithm.

- Consider whether food availability supports the intended session quality, subsequent recovery and the athlete's wider needs.
- Ask about fueling and energy intake when sessions repeatedly feel unusually difficult or performance deteriorates.
- Explain the uncertainty before suggesting a deliberate low-carbohydrate strategy, and evaluate its effect on actual training and adherence.
- Use separate, appropriate nutrition guidance for detailed intake recommendations.

## Limits on the takeaway

The interventions lasted only 1–4 weeks, used different strategies and included small, predominantly male samples. No detected overall benefit is not proof that benefit is impossible for every athlete. Conversely, a favorable molecular signal or a within-group change is insufficient to promise better performance. The review does not establish that fasted training is necessary, define an optimal carbohydrate dose, or test a complete long-term fueling strategy.

## Implication for a coaching application

Avoid automatically labeling fasted sessions as superior or selecting them solely because a ride is easy. Keep nutrition advice tied to session demands and athlete feedback; exact intake rules require their own evidence. These are proposed requirements, not claims about implemented repository behavior.
