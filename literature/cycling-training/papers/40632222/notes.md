# Heavy strength training effects on physiological determinants of endurance cyclist performance: a systematic review with meta-analysis

[Practical coach takeaways](takeaways.md)

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40632222/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-025-05883-2) · [Metadata](metadata.json) · [Abstract](abstract.txt)

**Evidence basis:** full paper pdf. Reviewed 22 October 2026.

## Why it belongs

A recent cycling-specific synthesis of heavy strength training as part of a broader endurance program.

## Findings — full paper

Seventeen studies included 262 cyclists, 60 female, over 5–25 weeks with 1–3 weekly strength sessions. The synthesis found small benefits for efficiency and moderate benefits for anaerobic power and cycling performance, without clear benefits for maximal oxygen uptake or maximal metabolic steady state. The authors rated certainty as **low** across outcomes. Different performance tests, including time trials and time-to-exhaustion tests, were pooled.

## Interpretation and limits

Small studies, varied interventions, additional versus substituted training, and low certainty constrain the prescription. Review registration occurred after the analyses. The paper's practical routine suggestions are author interpretation, not a meta-analytically established optimal dose. Adding strength work has recovery and opportunity costs, especially when time is fixed.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** offer appropriately progressed strength training according to goals, experience, equipment, and available recovery. Evaluate tolerability and whether cycling quality or adherence suffers. Heavy lifting requires suitable technique and progression.

**Not justified:** claiming guaranteed improvement, prescribing one routine to all cyclists, or treating a standardized effect size as a percentage improvement.
