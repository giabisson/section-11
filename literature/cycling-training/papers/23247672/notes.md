## Why it belongs

An authoritative ECSS/ACSM consensus for the balance between overload, recovery, health, and persistent underperformance. This is guidance informed by heterogeneous research and expert judgment, not an intervention meta-analysis.

## Findings — full paper

The statement distinguishes transient fatigue/functional overreaching from prolonged maladaptation. No single biomarker is accepted for diagnosing overtraining syndrome. Diagnosis requires clinical exclusion of other explanations, including illness and nutritional or medical problems. Coaching considerations include accurate records of actual training and performance, individualized intensity, attention to non-training stress, nutrition and sleep, adjustment for excessive fatigue, and recovery after illness/injury.

## Interpretation and limits

This is a 2013 consensus, not a current comprehensive return-to-sport guideline. Some mechanisms and diagnostic approaches were explicitly uncertain. Its clinical diagnostic framework should not become an autonomous chatbot diagnosis. Proposed cutoffs are not validated universal safety boundaries.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** symptoms and persistent performance decline should interrupt automatic load progression and trigger focused questions or appropriate clinical assessment. A favorable wearable score cannot rule out illness or injury. Preserve what actually happened rather than assuming prescribed load was completed.

**Equations and figures checked:** session-RPE load is duration in minutes × the specified session-RPE score (arbitrary units), with weekly sums. Monotony is mean daily load / its SD; strain is weekly load × monotony. Printed p. 199 / PDF p. 14 checked visually. These are descriptive monitoring constructs, not validated injury probabilities; zero SD makes monotony undefined. Figure 3 (PDF p. 13) and Tables 1–2 (p. 16) were checked. The table's >5% volume-increase item is part of an exclusion/assessment checklist, **not a proven maximum safe weekly ramp**. No numerical clinical triage rule is adopted here.
