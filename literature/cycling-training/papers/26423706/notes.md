## Why it belongs

A practical foundation for closing the coaching loop with athlete feedback rather than relying exclusively on wearable metrics.

## Findings — full PMC text, no original PDF

The systematic review included 56 studies with concurrent subjective and objective monitoring. Subjective well-being measures often responded more consistently to acute and chronic training changes; the two kinds of measure generally did not correlate. Several stress, fatigue, recovery, and well-being subscales were useful. Combining subscales into an overall score could reduce sensitivity.

## Interpretation and limits

This was heterogeneous, multisport evidence summarized without a meta-analysis. Responsiveness to training load is not the same as predicting injury or proving that an automated adjustment improves performance. Timing, recall window, individual baselines, questionnaire design, and honest reporting matter. **RPE was excluded from this review's eligible subjective measures**, so it should not be cited as validation of a custom exertion score or session-RPE equation.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** ask brief, consistent questions about fatigue, soreness, stress, recovery, and illness, with a defined reporting period. Keep useful individual items visible rather than hiding contradictory answers inside one score. Use them alongside actual training and performance observations.

**Not justified:** treating any single five-point “feel” response as a validated readiness index, or assuming self-report replaces all objective information.
