# Coach takeaways: Saw et al. (2016; online 2015): athlete self-report

**Paper:** Monitoring the athlete training response: subjective self-reported measures trump commonly used objective measures: a systematic review.

[Study / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-094758) · [Detailed assessment](notes.md) · [All paper takeaways](../../TAKEAWAYS.md)

**Evidence basis:** Full HTML/XML text reviewed; original PDF unavailable. Prepared 22 October 2026.

## Main coaching lesson

**Ask the athlete consistently how they are responding to training.**

## What the paper adds

This systematic review covered 56 multisport studies with concurrent subjective and objective monitoring. Subjective well-being measures often responded consistently to changes in training stress; subjective and objective measures generally did not correlate. Several fatigue, stress, recovery and well-being subscales were useful, while combining items into one total could reduce sensitivity.

## Coaching application

These are practical coaching interpretations, not a directly tested decision algorithm.

- Use a brief, repeatable check-in with a clear time window, asking about fatigue, soreness, stress, recovery and relevant symptoms.
- Compare responses with the athlete's usual pattern and ask about changes that could affect the next session.
- Keep individual responses available. A reassuring overall score can obscure one worsening item or a disagreement with performance data.
- Use feedback together with completed training and performance, and explain how it influenced the next recommendation.

## Limits on the takeaway

The studies were heterogeneous and the review did not perform a meta-analysis. Responsiveness to training does not establish injury prediction or prove that any particular automated adjustment improves performance. RPE was excluded from this review's eligible subjective measures, so it does not validate a custom exertion score or the session-RPE formula. A short bespoke check-in is a practical design choice, not automatically a validated questionnaire.

## Implication for a coaching application

Collect usable feedback and make it part of prescription → completion review → adjustment. Define each question and scale, preserve trends and important individual answers, and allow the athlete to explain unexpected changes. These are proposed requirements, not claims about implemented repository behavior.
