## Why it belongs

Tests whether an operational cycling estimate can stand in for a directly measured physiological intensity boundary.

## Findings — full paper, manually supplied

Thirteen cyclists (11 men, 2 women) with at least three years' experience and four weekly training hours completed physiological testing at FTP and FTP +15 W. The paper describes FTP as 95% of 20-minute time-trial power; the test used five minutes at 100 W before the 20-minute trial, without a described preceding five-minute maximal effort. FTP was determined on a Wahoo trainer; constant-load tests used a different laboratory ergometer.

Oxygen uptake stabilized at both intensities without reaching peak oxygen uptake. Seven of 13 reached the protocol's 40-minute cap at FTP; the others stopped earlier. Thus, the reported average of approximately 34 minutes is **not an uncensored mean time to exhaustion**. It should not be used to claim that every cyclist can hold FTP for only 34 minutes.

## Interpretation and limits

The results challenge identifying this FTP estimate with the heavy/severe boundary. They do not measure that boundary directly for every rider, validate a universal replacement multiplier, or establish that FTP has no practical training value. Device differences, small samples, the particular FTP protocol, and the capped tests constrain interpretation. The discussion shows that conclusions about maximal lactate steady state depend on the lactate criterion used; MLSS and the heavy/severe boundary must not be silently equated.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** record test method, date, equipment, and uncertainty; reconcile targets with sustained performance and effort. FTP, ramp peak power and critical power are different denominators.
