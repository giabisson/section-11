## Why it belongs

Connects training specificity to performance after accumulated work, which a fresh power test cannot fully describe.

## Findings — full paper

Twenty-one studies, totaling 585 participants, examined acute durability after prior work. Most samples were male and competitive/professional cyclists. Prior-work intensity, duration, athlete level, and testing method influenced subsequent performance. Mechanical work alone was insufficient to characterize fatigue. Studies differed substantially in feeding, prior work, and outcomes; critical power could remain stable while other performance measures deteriorated.

## Interpretation and limits

This is a review of acute performance impairment, **not a longitudinal trial of the best durability-training program**. Proposed training prescriptions are hypotheses or coaching interpretations. It does not establish that a short hard session is equivalent to a long ride, or supply a safe universal fatigue threshold in kJ.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** specify the demands of the target event, consider available longer rides when appropriate, and evaluate performance in relevant contexts. Interpret deterioration with pacing, fueling, environment, and measurement quality, rather than labeling every decline as lost fitness.

**Not justified:** deliberately exhausting every athlete to test durability, a universal kJ cutoff, or inferring that intensity can replace all volume.

**PDF check:** printed p. 176 / PDF p. 14 was visually inspected. The displayed relation power in watts × time in seconds yields **joules**. For constant power, mechanical work in kJ = power × seconds / 1000; variable-power data require integration/summation. This is mechanical work, not total metabolic energy or dietary calorie expenditure.
