## Why it belongs

A cycling-specific experiment frequently cited for polarized training, with useful details about actual prescription and progression.

## Findings — full paper, manually supplied; erratum unresolved

Twelve trained men entered a crossover experiment; **11 completed** after one injury withdrawal. Each six-week period was preceded by four weeks of reduced training, separated within a 29-week study. Polarized and threshold-focused periods averaged 381 and 458 minutes/week, respectively, with similar lower-intensity time but different total load.

The polarized arm used three weekly laboratory sessions of 6×4 minutes with two-minute recoveries, at power 5–10% above the individually determined lactate turnpoint. The threshold arm used 60 minutes between lactate threshold and turnpoint. Both added lower-intensity riding. Target power rose 5–10 W when specified effort/heart-rate responses decreased over two consecutive sessions. These are study-specific adjustments, not validated universal readiness rules.

Peak power, lactate-threshold power, and high-intensity time to exhaustion improved more after polarized training. Both arms improved 40-km performance; the between-model difference in 40-km mean-power improvement was **not statistically significant**. The 80/0/20 label used a session-goal approach, not an interchangeable ratio of measured heart-rate minutes or FTP zones.

## Interpretation and limits

The sample was small, the models differed in volume/load, and training history and preceding reduced intensity affect transferability. Three hard sessions per week in this experiment do not establish a safe default for all cyclists. The larger intensity-distribution synthesis should guide broader claims.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** retain easy work around demanding sessions and adjust load according to response. This study supports an effective option, not a compulsory 80/20 season plan.
