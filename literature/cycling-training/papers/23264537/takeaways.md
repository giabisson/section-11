# Coach takeaways: Neal et al. (2013): polarized versus threshold-focused cycling

**Paper:** Six weeks of a polarized training-intensity distribution leads to greater physiological and performance adaptations than a threshold model in trained cyclists.

[Study / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00652.2012) · [Detailed assessment](notes.md) · [All paper takeaways](../../TAKEAWAYS.md)

**Evidence basis:** Full paper assessed; selected original-PDF figures, tables or equations checked. See the detailed assessment for scope and unresolved issues. Prepared 22 October 2026.

## Main coaching lesson

**Use a purposeful mix of demanding and easy training, with progression informed by response.**

## What the paper adds

Eleven trained men completed a crossover comparison of two six-week periods, each preceded by reduced training. Polarized training improved peak power, lactate-threshold power and high-intensity time to exhaustion more than the threshold-focused comparator. Both periods improved 40-km performance; the between-model mean-power difference was not significant. The protocols differed in total volume/load, despite similar easy-training time.

## Coaching application

These are practical coaching interpretations, not a directly tested decision algorithm.

- Consider whether an athlete's recent training supplies the stimulus needed for their goal, rather than repeating the same moderate-effort pattern automatically.
- Plan easier work and recovery around demanding sessions, taking total training and non-training demands into account.
- Use consistent performance and effort observations to decide when to progress. The study's 5–10 W adjustments were a particular protocol, not a general readiness rule.

## Limits on the takeaway

The sample was small and the intervention short. The polarized arm included three weekly laboratory sessions of 6×4-minute intervals, which is not a universal safe default. Its 80/0/20 description used a session-goal approach and cannot be directly substituted for measured heart-rate minutes or FTP-zone time. Improvements in selected physiological outcomes do not establish superiority for every event or a whole-season plan.

## Implication for a coaching application

Compare prescribed training with completion and response before adjusting future load. Keep the workout dose, intensity classification and progression rationale visible instead of enforcing an unexplained 80/20 rule. These are proposed requirements, not claims about implemented repository behavior.
