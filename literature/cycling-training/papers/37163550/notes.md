# Effects of tapering on performance in endurance athletes: A systematic review and meta-analysis

[Practical coach takeaways](takeaways.md)

Wang Z, Wang YT, Gao W, Zhong Y. Effects of tapering on performance in endurance athletes: A systematic review and meta-analysis. PLoS One. 2023;18(5):e0282838. doi: 10.1371/journal.pone.0282838

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37163550/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0282838) · [Metadata](metadata.json) · [Abstract](abstract.txt)

**Evidence basis:** full paper pdf. Reviewed 22 October 2026.

## Why it belongs

Adds event preparation to the evidence base, beyond designing training sessions and ordinary loading weeks.

## Findings — full paper

Fourteen studies included 174 endurance athletes, mostly male runners and cyclists. Tapering improved time-trial performance in pre/post comparisons; experimental versus control comparisons also favored tapering (SMD −0.73, 95% CI −1.25 to −0.21). A 41–60% volume-reduction subgroup had a favorable effect, and the 8–14-day subgroup had the largest duration estimate.

## Interpretation and limits

Subgroup precision is limited: only 18 participants contributed to the 8–14-day category. Table 2 reports no significant between-subgroup differences for intensity (p = .10), volume (.32), frequency (.42), or taper type (.60); duration differed (.02). A significant effect in one subgroup and a nonsignificant effect in another does not establish that the settings differ. Narrative text on PDF p. 8 incorrectly labels decreased-frequency results p < .05; Table 2 gives p = .16 and a confidence interval crossing zero. The reduction categories also overlap at exactly 60% in the printed labels. Findings do not justify algorithmic precision.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** before a priority event, consider reducing accumulated fatigue while retaining some familiar intensity, with duration and volume changes individualized to preceding load and response.

**Not justified:** mandatory overload before every event, a universally optimal 41–60% reduction, or transferring event-taper evidence directly to a fixed monthly deload schedule.

**PDF checks:** Figures 3–4 on PDF p. 8 and Table 2 on p. 9 visually inspected. The overload comparison is small and subgroup-based; it is not permission to deliberately overreach every athlete.
