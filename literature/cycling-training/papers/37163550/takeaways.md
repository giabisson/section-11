# Coach takeaways: Wang et al. (2023): tapering before an event

**Paper:** Effects of tapering on performance in endurance athletes: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

[Study / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0282838) · [Detailed assessment](notes.md) · [All paper takeaways](../../TAKEAWAYS.md)

**Evidence basis:** Full paper assessed; selected original-PDF figures, tables or equations checked. See the detailed assessment for scope and unresolved issues. Prepared 22 October 2026.

## Main coaching lesson

**Plan a reduction in accumulated fatigue before a priority event, adapted to the athlete.**

## What the paper adds

Fourteen studies included 174 endurance athletes, mainly male runners and cyclists. Tapering improved time-trial performance in both pre/post and controlled comparisons. Subgroups suggested useful approaches, including maintaining intensity while reducing volume, but most comparisons between alternative settings were not significant. The largest duration estimate came from only 18 participants in the 8–14-day category.

## Coaching application

These are practical coaching interpretations, not a directly tested decision algorithm.

- Identify priority events and consider the actual training load and fatigue leading into them.
- Use a feasible reduction in volume while considering some familiar intensity, then adjust according to recovery and event demands.
- Observe how the athlete responds and use that experience to improve future tapers.
- Distinguish preparation for a priority event from an ordinary recovery adjustment during training.

## Limits on the takeaway

The commonly quoted 41–60% volume reduction is a useful research reference, not a proven optimum for everyone. Table 2 did not establish significant between-subgroup differences for intensity, volume, frequency or taper type; duration differed but estimates were imprecise. The review does not justify mandatory overload before a taper or a fixed monthly deload rule. Some narrative p-values and category labels are inconsistent.

## Implication for a coaching application

Connect taper decisions to an event date, recent completed training and athlete response. State the reference volume and reasoning for the proposed reduction; avoid mechanically applying one percentage to every rider. These are proposed requirements, not claims about implemented repository behavior.
