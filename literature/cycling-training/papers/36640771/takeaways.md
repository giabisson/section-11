# Coach takeaways: Galán-Rioja et al. (2023): training periodization

**Paper:** Training Periodization, Intensity Distribution, and Volume in Trained Cyclists: A Systematic Review.

[Study / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2022-0302) · [Detailed assessment](notes.md) · [All paper takeaways](../../TAKEAWAYS.md)

**Evidence basis:** Full paper assessed; selected original-PDF figures, tables or equations checked. See the detailed assessment for scope and unresolved issues. Prepared 22 October 2026.

## Main coaching lesson

**Use a coherent training structure that can change with the athlete's response.**

## What the paper adds

Seven studies covered 161 cyclists, including only 10 women. Most comparisons lasted 8–12 weeks; a 58-week report followed a single athlete. Block, traditional and monitoring-led day-to-day approaches could improve fitness, without a consistently superior model. The main contribution is the distinction between arranging several weeks of sessions and demonstrating an effective whole-season plan.

## Coaching application

These are practical coaching interpretations, not a directly tested decision algorithm.

- Start with the athlete's goal, event demands, training background and available time. Give each block a purpose, then select sessions that serve it.
- Make progression and recovery explicit, with a review point to assess what the athlete actually completed and how they responded.
- Allow the weekly structure to change for fatigue, missed training or changing availability while keeping the longer-term objective visible.

## Limits on the takeaway

The review does not establish one best annual model, a mandatory three-week build/one-week recovery cycle, or a universal weekly load ramp. Its suggested training-hour range describes a particular population and includes author judgment; it is not a minimum requirement for benefiting from training. Small, heterogeneous studies and sparse seasonal comparisons limit confidence. Some aggregate percentages and screening counts are inconsistent in the original paper.

## Implication for a coaching application

Store block objectives, intended progression, recovery opportunities and review dates. Evaluate whether the sequence remains coherent after changes. A set of evidence-informed sessions alone does not demonstrate that the resulting plan works. These are proposed requirements, not claims about implemented repository behavior.
