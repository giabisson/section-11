## Why it belongs

A recent synthesis using individual participant data, useful for testing strong claims about polarized versus pyramidal training.

## Findings — full paper

Thirteen studies included 348 athletes (296 male, 52 female) across five endurance sports. Only two studies were exclusively cycling, with additional mixed-sport evidence. Interventions lasted 3–18 weeks. No clear overall advantage of polarized over pyramidal training was established for maximal oxygen uptake or time-trial performance. Per-protocol comparisons reported SMD −0.06 for oxygen uptake and −0.05 for time trials, with confidence intervals crossing zero.

Training level showed a subgroup interaction. Crucially, the competitive and recreational contrasts each remained nonsignificant (p = .13 and .18). Figure 4's interaction p = .04 does not establish a winning model within either subgroup. Changes in oxygen uptake were not reliably associated with changes in time-trial performance in this dataset.

## Interpretation and limits

Actual heart-rate time in zone reclassified 67 participants (19%) relative to intended distribution. A session-goal ratio cannot be equated to the same ratio of minutes in heart-rate zones. The paper includes short interventions, limited female representation, some studies at high risk of bias, and no registered review protocol.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** permit several distributions, record the classification method, and assess completion, recovery, and goal-specific performance. Do not market polarized or pyramidal training as universally best.
