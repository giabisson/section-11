# Training Prescription Guided by Heart-Rate Variability in Cycling

Javaloyes A, Sarabia JM, Lamberts RP, Moya-Ramon M. Training Prescription Guided by Heart-Rate Variability in Cycling. Int J Sports Physiol Perform. 2019;14(1):23-32. doi: 10.1123/ijspp.2018-0122

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29809080/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2018-0122) · [Metadata](metadata.json) · [Abstract](abstract.txt)

**Evidence basis:** abstract only. Reviewed 22 October 2026.

## Why it belongs

A direct cycling test of adjusting prescription using heart-rate variability rather than following only a predetermined schedule.

## Findings — abstract only

Seventeen well-trained cyclists established resting HRV over four baseline weeks, then followed eight weeks of HRV-guided or traditional training. Peak power, power at the second ventilatory threshold, and 40-minute performance improved within the HRV group. **Between-group differences were not significant.** The abstract's positive conclusion also relies on magnitude-based inference, which should not be treated as a confirmed superiority result.

## Interpretation and limits

Improvement within one group and nonsignificant improvement in another do not establish that the groups improved differently. Small samples make meaningful uncertainty especially important. The original decision rules, missing-measurement handling, allocation, and adherence have not been checked in full.

The Manresa-Rocamora review's Table 2 describes related cycling protocols with standardized morning recordings, rolling seven-value averages, and reference ranges based on a baseline. Those are secondary descriptions, not a substitute for this paper's original methods, and not evidence for an arbitrary combined readiness formula.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** collect consistent HRV observations if available, interpret trends against an individual baseline, and combine them with performance, symptoms, and athlete feedback.

**Not justified:** automatic permission to train hard because HRV is high, hard cancellation thresholds copied without measurement context, or claims of proven performance superiority.
