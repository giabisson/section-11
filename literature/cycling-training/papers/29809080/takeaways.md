# Coach takeaways: Javaloyes et al. (2019): HRV-guided training

**Paper:** Training Prescription Guided by Heart-Rate Variability in Cycling.

[Study / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2018-0122) · [Detailed assessment](notes.md) · [All paper takeaways](../../TAKEAWAYS.md)

**Evidence basis:** Abstract only; provisional. The original full paper remains unavailable. Prepared 22 October 2026.

## Main coaching lesson

**Treat this paper as provisional support for investigating HRV-guided prescription.**

## What the paper adds

According to the abstract, 17 well-trained cyclists established resting HRV over four weeks, then completed eight weeks of HRV-guided or traditional training. Several outcomes improved within the HRV-guided group, but the abstract reports no significant between-group differences. Its favorable interpretation also uses magnitude-based inference.

## Coaching application

These are practical coaching interpretations, not a directly tested decision algorithm.

- Discuss HRV as an optional source of information about the athlete's response, with uncertainty made explicit.
- Interpret it alongside completed training, performance and athlete feedback rather than allowing one number to determine the whole plan.
- Use the available broader HRV review and the separate 2020 and 2025 full papers to inform the assessment while maintaining their distinct populations and comparators.

## Limits on the takeaway

This takeaway is based on the abstract only. Allocation, exact decision rules, adherence, missing-data handling and full outcome analysis remain unverified. Improvement in one group and a nonsignificant change in another do not prove a difference between groups. The separately supplied 2020 study is a different experiment; Alfonso 2025 is also not a replacement for this paper's original methods.

## Implication for a coaching application

Do not claim that this citation validates the application's readiness score or thresholds. Any HRV-based decision rule needs its own stated inputs, measurement requirements, uncertainty and validation. These are proposed requirements, not claims about implemented repository behavior.
