## Findings — full paper, manually supplied

Twenty well-trained cyclists were recruited; 15 completed (one injury withdrawal, four excluded for training/HRV noncompliance). After a two-week baseline, eight weeks of HRV-guided prescription were compared with a predetermined block program. Both groups averaged approximately 11 hours/week. Fitness and 40-minute power improved on some within-group tests, but **between-group fitness/performance differences were not established**, including by the authors' magnitude-based inference analysis.

Morning measurements used smartphone photoplethysmography, supine positioning, 90 seconds including 30 seconds of stabilization, and the last 60 seconds for analysis. The protocol used a seven-day rolling average of log-transformed RMSSD, a baseline reference band of mean ±0.5 SD, and an update after four training weeks. Figure 2 shows a training-decision sequence, rather than a validated standalone readiness number.

## Interpretation and limits

Small samples, compliance-based exclusions, limited reporting of allocation, and a demanding comparator limit inference. The Methods assign HRV n = 8/BP n = 7, but Tables 1–2 reverse these labels. Report 15 completers and preserve the unresolved assignment discrepancy. Some narrative superiority claims exceed the reported between-group results.

The printed TRIMP equation says time in seconds “between VT2 and VT2” and omits an explicit high-zone multiplier; these errors are present in the original, not merely MinerU. Do not silently repair it or use it to reproduce the reported training loads. An original reference would be needed for any calculation.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** optional HRV trends can inform timing alongside symptoms, training and performance. This study does not validate arbitrary cutoffs, a composite readiness score, or using normal HRV to dismiss illness.
