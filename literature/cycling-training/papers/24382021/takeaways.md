# Coach takeaways: Rønnestad et al. (2015; online 2014): short versus long intervals

**Paper:** Short intervals induce superior training adaptations compared with long intervals in cyclists - an effort-matched approach.

[Study / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12165) · [Detailed assessment](notes.md) · [All paper takeaways](../../TAKEAWAYS.md)

**Evidence basis:** Full paper assessed; selected original-PDF figures, tables or equations checked. See the detailed assessment for scope and unresolved issues. Prepared 22 October 2026.

## Main coaching lesson

**Specify the complete interval dose when choosing a short-interval session.**

## What the paper adds

Sixteen male cyclists completed ten weeks comparing twice-weekly short and long intervals. The short format used three sets of 13×30 seconds with 15-second within-set recoveries; Methods/Table 2 give three minutes between sets. The comparator was 4×5 minutes with 2.5-minute recoveries. The short format produced greater gains in maximal oxygen uptake and peak aerobic power, while several other between-group outcomes were uncertain.

## Coaching application

These are practical coaching interpretations, not a directly tested decision algorithm.

- Consider short intervals as an option when repeated hard efforts suit the session objective and the athlete's preparation.
- Check the number of sets and repetitions, all recoveries and total session time when fitting the workout into a week.
- Assess whether the athlete sustains useful power and recovers adequately. Similar perceived effort does not mean identical work, energy expenditure or session duration.

## Limits on the takeaway

This was a small comparison of two particular formats, with four withdrawals. It does not show that every short-interval workout beats every long-interval workout. The narrative reports p = .056 for the between-group 40-minute improvement, despite stronger figure markings. Published recovery descriptions also conflict: Methods/Table 2 use three-minute set rests, while Perspectives says 2.5 minutes. Table 2 gives 34.5 minutes for the interval block, excluding warm-up/down; it includes recovery omitted by the narrative's nine-minute total.

## Implication for a coaching application

Calculate workout duration from its actual steps and preserve unresolved source discrepancies. A generic '30/15' label is insufficient for reproducing this intervention or estimating its load. These are proposed requirements, not claims about implemented repository behavior.
