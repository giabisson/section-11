## Why it belongs

A direct cycling comparison of interval organization, useful for choosing session options with explicit dosing and uncertainty.

## Findings — full paper, manually supplied

Twenty male cyclists were randomized; 16 completed ten weeks (9 short-interval, 7 long-interval). Both groups completed two demanding sessions weekly, with lower-intensity riding between them. Short intervals comprised three sets of **13×30 seconds**, with 15-second recoveries within sets. Methods and Table 2 specify three minutes between sets. Long intervals were 4×5 minutes with 2.5-minute recoveries. Recovery power was 50% of work power, with maximal sustainable session effort.

Maximal oxygen uptake and peak aerobic power improved more in the short-interval group. Other outcomes need more cautious wording: the narrative reports p = .12 for the between-group change in power at 4 mmol/L and p = .056 for 40-minute performance. Favorable effect sizes do not make all comparisons statistically significant. Effort and hard-work duration were similar; session duration and mechanical work were not identical.

## Interpretation and limits

This is a small completer analysis with four withdrawals and a specific comparator, not proof that every short interval beats every long interval. Hypothesized muscular mechanisms were not directly tested with tissue analysis.

**Original-paper inconsistencies:** Table 2 calculates 900 seconds (15 minutes) of total recovery and 2070 seconds (34.5 minutes) for the short-interval block, excluding warm-up/down. The narrative's nine-minute recovery figure excludes the six minutes between sets despite calling it total recovery. The Perspectives section gives 2.5-minute between-set recovery, conflicting with Methods/Table 2. Figure significance markings are also stronger than some narrative p-values. Preserve these discrepancies rather than silently manufacturing one definitive protocol.

**Reasonable coaching judgment:** short intervals are a useful option; choose tolerable power and progression from session response. Do not describe every endpoint or the complete regimen as universally superior.
